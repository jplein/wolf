# Retrospective: keys reach a Neovim prompt while a pump call waits on it

Last verified at commit `7d4d041a2f37baf7e2023611a955b7312c666b84`.

The task fixed a window that froze when Neovim raised a prompt inside an input-pump call: W12 from the refocus call `nvim_ui_set_focus`, or `:write`'s y/n from GNOME's Save. The fix moved the pump into `crates/wolf-core/src/pump.rs`, which releases held keys while a call waits on input. It took two rounds and ten invocations for $28.95: round 1 cost $22.11, and review 1's rejection added a round of $6.84.

## What went well

- **Every path into Neovim went through one pump and one translation module.** Both front-ends end in `send_input`, and every RPC an event issues was in `input.rs`, so plan 1 routed them all through one `Rpc` trait with no front-end change. `architect-1` confirmed it by tracing `keyDown`, `applicationDidBecomeActive`, `windowDidBecomeKey` and GNOME's `save_buffer`, and review 1 found no path the plan missed.
- **The snapshot already exposed the prompt.** `toast.is_prompt()` and `cmdline.prompt` let the tests wait on each prompt and made `prompt_showing` one line. No test needed a new seam.
- **The shared harness made "hang until the deadline" free.** `common::poll_until` with `FLUSH_TIMEOUT` turns a key stuck behind its call into a panic at 10 s, the failure the task's falsifier asks for. `coder-1`'s red run showed cases 1–5 and 7 failing at the deadline on macOS, and five of six `prompt_input` cases on Linux.
- **A sibling target was there to copy.** The `prompt_input` `rust_test` mirrors `autoread` in `crates/wolf-core/BUILD.bazel`, and review 1 found its runfiles, `WOLF_*` env and `no-sandbox` tag right on the first pass.
- **Measuring Neovim before planning settled the cause.** The instructions' lead was `InputEvent::Save`, but macOS has no Save path. `architect-1` drove Neovim 0.12.6 with a raw msgpack client and found W12 inside the focus call, the 2 s focus throttle behind "only sometimes", and the chord case. Every premise held: no test was rewritten in either round, and recommendation 6 is what would keep this cheap.

## What did not go well

- **The one round trip: review 1 found a freeze plan 1 designed in.** Plan 1 held every key behind a held `Paste`, and cleared the stall at the budget while a prompt showed. Together, a paste typed over W12 froze the window with no stall overlay (review 1's C1), while the spec text plan 1 dictated promised that no call holds back the answering key (V1). Round 2, `architect-2` to `reviewer-2`, cost $6.84, 123 turns and 26 minutes.
- **Nothing in the repository said which events Neovim takes through a fast call.** `is_typing` groups `Paste` with keys, and plan 1 built its release rule on it and special-cased paste, though `nvim_paste` is not fast. The task added `InputEvent::is_fast_input` with that meaning, so this needs no further change.
- **The coder knew two of review 1's findings and returned `passed`.** `coder-1`'s report listed, as "Known gaps, for the reviewer", that a key can overtake a paste that is the waiting call (C2), and that a probe can reach Neovim first when another task holds nvim-rs's writer lock (C3). It still wrote plan 1's "typing keeps its order" into `specs/command-lifecycle.md`. Its definition says that whatever needs somebody to act is a `rejected`.
- **Neither technical writer read the coder's report.** The implementation gate sent both, so each header named a gate log, and `technical-writer.md` never names `reports/coder-<N>.md`. `technical-writer-1` then wrote "the probe follows it to Neovim" and "typing keeps its order" into the `pump.rs` README entry, review 1's V2 and half its V1. `coder-1`'s report stated both exceptions, in a section addressed to the technical writer.
- **The stated base is the request's, and four invocations re-derived the task's own.** The mechanics named `6abafa3`, nine tasks back. `coder-1`'s F1 first restored the fix's files from it, `nvim_liveness` failed to compile, and it re-ran F1 from `582cb41`. `reviewer-1`, `architect-2`, whose plan 2 says "Diff against `582cb41`", and `reviewer-2` each found that commit by reading the parent chain.
- **zsh ate `coder-1`'s first F1.** In `"$B:crates/…"` zsh read `:c` as a history modifier, so the revert wrote empty files and nothing ran. The coder's definition warns about zsh's `=` word only, and plan 2 had to restate the `${B}` form for `coder-2`.
- **Four invocations hunted for nvim-rs's source.** `architect-1` searched `~/.cargo`, `/nix/store` and `/private/var/tmp`, then fetched `neovim.rs` from docs.rs. `architect-2`, `coder-1` and `technical-writer-2` each ran `find` until they reached `rules_rust++crate+crates__nvim-rs-0.9.2` under the task's Bazel output root.
- **The pinned Neovim has no documented path on macOS.** `build.md` exports `WOLF_NVIM_BIN` in the Linux shell only, and `architect-1`'s `echo $WOLF_NVIM_BIN` in the macOS dev shell printed nothing. It listed `/nix/store`, which held at least six Neovim builds of 0.12.5 and 0.12.6, picked one by version, and found a Python with `msgpack` the same way. `build.md` says never to find Neovim by scanning `/nix/store`, and names no binary a scratch probe should use.
- **Plan 1 gave a gotcha to a README entry with no room.** The `input.rs` entry in `crates/wolf-core/src/README.md` stood at 250 of 250 words at the start. `technical-writer-1` learned that by rerunning the checker with `WOLF_MD_MAX_ENTRY_WORDS=10`, moved the `Rpc` gotcha to `pump.rs` and trimmed two bullets. The new `pump.rs` entry ended round 1 at 248 words and round 2 at 250, and `architect-2` counted it with its own `awk`.
- **The budget cost the landed entry a fact.** To fit, `technical-writer-2` dropped plan 2's "State events are never released" from the `pump.rs` entry, which review 2 noted. The spec, `releasable`'s doc and `notes/wolf-core-decisions.md` still state it.
- **`crates/wolf-core/tests/README.md` is past the size warning.** It grew to 51298 bytes in round 1 and 51336 in round 2, against the checker's 50000, and both reviews flagged it.
- **Core's stall rule is restated in wolf-gnome's documents.** "A typed batch outlasts the budget" was written out twice in `notes/wolf-gnome-decisions.md`, once in `crates/wolf-gnome/tests/README.md`, and in `liveness.rs`'s doc comments. Plan 1 §5 named one of the four places. `technical-writer-1` found two more by grep and `coder-1` the doc comments, and each was rewritten.

## Recommendations

1. **Have the technical writer read the coder's report.** In `.claude/skills/regina/agents/technical-writer.md`, under `reason: zero`, name the latest `reports/coder-<N>.md` as input, above all its known gaps and the documents it says the change makes wrong. Both technical writers here skipped it, and it held review 1's V2 and half its V1.
2. **Say that a gap against a document the coder writes is a dispute.** In `.claude/skills/regina/agents/coder.md`, beside "Whatever needs somebody to act is a `rejected`", add that code narrower than a spec sentence the plan has the coder write goes back to the architect, not into known gaps. `coder-1` wrote "typing keeps its order" while listing the case that breaks it, and a rejection then would have reached the architect before two invocations built on the claim.
3. **Record the task's start commit once.** In `.claude/skills/regina/agents/architect.md`, have plan 1 state `git rev-parse HEAD` as the task's start. In `coder.md` and `reviewer.md`, say that a falsifier reverts to it and a review diffs against it, not the base the mechanics name. `coder-1`'s first F1 failed on this, and three more invocations re-derived `582cb41`.
4. **Warn about zsh's `:` modifiers.** In the zsh paragraph of the six definitions under `.claude/skills/regina/agents/`, add that `$B:c` applies a history modifier, so a variable before a colon is written `${B}:`. It cost `coder-1` a failed F1, and plan 2 restated it by hand.
5. **Say where a pinned crate's source is.** This came up again: it is recommendation 5 of [round-3-9](round-3-9.md), recommendation 6 of [round-3-8](round-3-8.md) and recommendation 4 of [open-items-13](open-items-13.md). Here four invocations found nvim-rs's source, three by `find` and one on docs.rs.
6. **Name the binary a scratch Neovim probe uses on macOS.** In `build.md`'s macOS section, say how to reach the pinned Neovim, for example `nvim/bin/nvim` under the path `bazel cquery --output=files //bazel/nvim:nvim_tree` prints. Add `msgpack` for Python to the macOS dev shell in `flake.nix`, or name the interpreter to use. `architect-1` spent four calls on `/nix/store` scans and chose among at least six builds by version.
7. **Report entry word counts, and split the over-budget READMEs.** This came up again: it is recommendation 3 of [open-items-12](open-items-12.md), recommendation 3 of [round-3-7](round-3-7.md) and recommendation 6 of [round-3-9](round-3-9.md). Here plan 1 sent a gotcha to a full entry, two agents counted words by hand, a new entry lost a fact to the budget, and `crates/wolf-core/tests/README.md` passed 50000 bytes.
8. **Link core's stall rule from wolf-gnome instead of restating it.** In `notes/wolf-gnome-decisions.md`, the paragraphs beginning "Core marks a stall" and "Nothing in this crate clears", and the `NotResponding` gap in `crates/wolf-gnome/tests/README.md`, keep what is GNOME's and link [`notes/wolf-core-decisions.md#liveness-and-restart`](../wolf-core-decisions.md#liveness-and-restart) for the rule. This task had to find and rewrite all three, and plan 1 named only one.
