# Retrospective: find and fix the Linux gate's `FLUSH_TIMEOUT` failures

Last verified at commit `c93431a71d3ce6334ca38791af4992c67b73d67a`.

The task asked for the cause of the real-Neovim `FLUSH_TIMEOUT` failures under the Linux gate, found by measurement, and a fix. The cause was the `build` step's writeback: 9–17 GB of dirty pages whose 45–100 s drain stalls file operations under `TMPDIR` in the kernel for 25 s or more. The fix is one `run writeback sync` step between `build` and `test` in `gates.sh`'s Linux branch, with a sentence on `FLUSH_TIMEOUT`'s doc comment and the measurement in `notes/gates.sh.md`.

It took six invocations, about $11.03 and 105 minutes of agent time: `architect-1` $4.46, `coder-1` $2.17 (70 minutes), `architect-2` $1.28, `coder-2` $0.62, `technical-writer-1` $0.72 and `reviewer-1` $1.78. Every gate passed on its first run on both workers. The one round trip was a `rejected` that plan 1 itself asked for.

This task carries out recommendation 4 of [tabs-and-preview-5](tabs-and-preview-5.md), 5 of [close-commands-2-2](close-commands-2-2.md) and 1 of [close-commands-2-3](close-commands-2-3.md). A cleanup round can mark them done.

## What went well

- **`gates.sh`'s `run <name> <command>` shape made the fix one line.** `coder-2` added `run writeback sync` and a two-line comment, and the step got its own log and timing for free. The Linux gate printed `writeback 0` between `build 0` and `test 0`, and `target/gates/writeback.log` ended `gates.sh: writeback exited 0 after 85s`, which the coder and the reviewer quoted without any extra instrumentation.
- **`regina on linux` kept `target/` between commands and let the snapshot carry scratch edits.** `coder-1` ran 17 remote commands: facts, four loads, a `/proc` sampler, the R0 batch and the prototype batch. Plan 1 §2 told it to apply every measurement edit inside the remote command, as [close-commands-2-3](close-commands-2-3.md) recommendation 4 asks, and the local workspace stayed clean through the whole measurement round.
- **The three earlier reports named each failing case with its timings.** The task instructions could list the six cases and their loaded and unloaded times, and `coder-1` matched the drawer suite's stretch to 15.08 s in R0 cycle 8 against close-commands-2-3's recorded 14.83 s. That tied the unfailed drawer cases to the same cause without a separate batch.
- **Plan 2 foresaw doc-lint's ordering between the coder and the writer.** It had both new comments link the existing `notes/gates.sh.md#linux-gates` heading rather than a new one, because `md-anchor` would fail the coder's gate on a heading the writer has not yet written, and `notes-unlinked` would fail the documentation gate the other way. Both gates passed first time on both workers.

## What did not go well

- **The one round trip was a planned `rejected` whose fix turned out to be the coder's own.** Plan 1 let `coder-1` change a file only for outcome C, a larger `FLUSH_TIMEOUT`, and told it to return `rejected` for any other cause, on the guess that such a fix would land in a test file. The measured fix was in `gates.sh`, a coder file, and `coder-1` had already prototyped and measured it at 10 of 10. The rejection added `architect-2` ($1.28, 4 minutes) and a second coder invocation, `coder-2` ($0.62, 9 minutes), which made a two-line edit and re-ran the gates.
- **Plan 1's leads rested on facts about the Linux worker that no document recorded and the architect could not check.** `architect-1` spent $4.46 and 52 turns, 40% of the task's cost, reading `pump.rs`, `render.rs`, `api.rs`, `init.lua` and Neovim's `'fsync'` help to build lead 1, fsync on a disk-backed `/tmp`. `coder-1`'s first command showed `/tmp` is an overlay mounted `fsync=volatile`, where fsync costs 0.6 ms, so plan 1's batches B1 and B2 were moot. The architect has no `regina on linux`, so it could not run the `stat -f` and `/proc/mounts` reads that settled this.
- **The earlier reports and the task framed the cause as test parallelism, and it was not.** The three reports above said the waits "race the gate's parallel `cargo test`", and plan 1 lead 2 followed them. `coder-1`'s CPU-only load, 64 busy loops on 32 CPUs, failed 0 of 10, while writeback alone failed 1 of 10 and the gate's build-then-test shape 5 of 10. Those reports are history and stay as written; the cause is the step before `test`, not the tests' own concurrency.
- **The Linux dev shell lacks the tools a stall investigation needs.** `coder-1`'s first command found no `strace`, `stress-ng`, `free` or `pgrep`, so it wrote a `python3` sampler of `/proc/<pid>/task/<tid>/wchan` to find the blocking calls. With the 10-minute command limit, each 10-cycle batch also took three commands, and `coder-1` ran 70 minutes.
- **Two figures in the landed note are inferences stated as measurements.** Review 1 §2 found that `notes/gates.sh.md` says the 9–17 GB of dirty pages are "under `target/`", while the measured figure is the host-wide `Dirty` of `/proc/meminfo`. "data=ordered" is ext4's default, also inferred, since `/proc/mounts` shows only `rw,relatime`.
- **`sync` covers only the writeback that exists when it runs.** Review 1 §2 notes that dirty data another process writes on the shared ext4 during `test`, such as another task's build on the same host, is not flushed by the gate. If a real-Neovim `timed out after 10s` comes back during the Linux `test` step, that is the first lead to check.

## Recommendations

1. **Let the architect run read-only probes on the Linux worker.** This extends recommendation 1 of [application-state-10](application-state-10.md) and of [close-commands-2-1](close-commands-2-1.md), come up again, from a scratch Neovim to the secondary worker. In `.claude/skills/regina/agents/architect.md`, allow `regina on linux` for commands that only read, such as `stat -f`, `/proc/mounts` and `nvim --headless -c 'echo &fsync'`. Plan 1 would then have dropped its fsync lead and the B1 and B2 batches before handing off, and saved part of `architect-1`'s $4.46.
2. **In a measure-then-fix plan, let the coder apply any measured fix that lands in a coder-owned file.** In `.claude/skills/regina/agents/architect.md`, where a plan branches on an outcome it cannot know yet, say that the coder carries out a fix in its own files once its batch passes, and returns `rejected` only for a test-writer file, a production bug or a spec. Here that would have closed the task in `coder-1`, saving `architect-2` ($1.28) and the second coder round.
3. **Record the Linux worker's filesystem facts where a planner looks first.** `notes/gates.sh.md`'s "Temporary directory" section says where `TMPDIR` goes but not what it is on regina's Linux secondary. One sentence there, that `/tmp` is a `fsync=volatile` overlay on the same ext4 as `target/` and that the worker has no usable tmpfs, would have told `architect-1` what `coder-1`'s first command found; link it from the new "Linux gates" paragraph rather than restating it.
4. **Add `strace` and `procps` to the Linux dev shell in `flake.nix`.** `coder-1` had to hand-write a `/proc` sampler because `strace`, `free` and `pgrep` were missing, and plan 1 §6 step 3 had planned on `strace` for B3. Both are small and Linux-only, and would make the next stall a one-line trace.
5. **Correct the two inferred figures in `notes/gates.sh.md`'s "Linux gates".** Say "host-wide dirty pages, mostly the relinked test binaries" instead of "under `target/`", and drop "data=ordered" or mark it as ext4's default, per review 1 §2. The note is the record a later agent sizes any change to `FLUSH_TIMEOUT` or the `writeback` step against.
