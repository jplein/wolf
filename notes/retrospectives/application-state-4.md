# Retrospective: the application-state model, its SQLite store and the wolf-ffi surface

Last verified at commit `76c9cde181e76da88b8e1a272537da5bd062cf31`.

The task added `crates/wolf-core/src/app_state.rs` (the §2 model of `specs/application-state.md`, the §4 selection rule and the §5 restore helpers), `crates/wolf-core/src/app_state_store.rs` (`AppStateStore` over the `window_state` and `project_state` tables of `wolf.sqlite`), a state class with migrations and a sticky newer flag in `db.rs`, a cursor and top-line restore in `runtime/nvim/init.lua`, and a builder, submit and read surface in `crates/wolf-ffi/src/lib.rs`. No front-end changed.

The five invocations cost about $26.74 and 87 minutes of agent time: `architect-1` $6.56 (22 min), `test-writer-1` $4.12 (13 min), `coder-1` $6.58 (29 min), `technical-writer-1` $4.94 (16 min) and `reviewer-1` $4.54 (8 min). There was no round trip. Every agent ran once, `implementation-1` and `documentation-1` passed on both workers on their first run, and review 1 approved with minor notes.

## What went well

- **Plan 1 §2 gave the exact public surface, and the test writer type-checked against it.** `test-writer-1` ran `cargo clippy --test <each>` on the Linux worker over a throwaway stub of that surface, passed inline and never written to the workspace. `coder-1`'s first `bazel test` of the five new targets compiled and passed, and review 1 §6 found that it edited no test file.
- **Headless real-SQLite targets kept 60 falsifiers cheap.** `app_state_model`, `app_state_rules` and `app_state_store` copy the headless `:db` target, so `coder-1` scripted all 60 falsifiers, each with a restore checksum, and its transcript shows the pass running from 23:45 to 23:52, about seven minutes. This is the strength [application-state-1](application-state-1.md) recorded for `tests/db.rs`. (`coder-1`'s report says the pass took about 30 minutes, which its transcript does not bear out.)
- **Named sibling targets made the wiring mechanical.** Plan 1 §3 told the test writer to copy `:db`, `:rebuild_layout` and `//crates/wolf-ffi:state`, and `CLAUDE.md` lists what a real-Neovim target needs. Review 1 §3 found all five targets wired, and the macOS gate's test log lists every one as `PASSED`.
- **Existing tests guarded the shared-builder change in `state.rs`.** `spec_value` builds through `app_state::leaf_spec` and `layout_spec`, and `tests/state_layout.rs` and `tests/rebuild_layout.rs` confirmed that today's rebuild document did not change, with no new test needed.

## What did not go well

No rework happened, so nothing below cost a round trip. The findings are drift that landed and costs inside single invocations.

- **Three depth-first pane walks have to agree, and no test nests a layout.** `Node::panes` (`crates/wolf-core/src/app_state.rs:165`), `replace_missing` (`:444`) and `node_spec` (`:491`) each number panes on their own, and `PaneRef::Pane(i)` means the same index in all three. Review 1 §3 gap 1 found that every `restored_tab` and `tab_rebuild_spec` test uses a flat layout, so a walk that numbered a layout's direct children would pass. The front-ends start writing these indices in the next tasks of this series.
- **The instructions' task-series wording landed in code.** The instructions say the selection rule is what "later tasks use … both for saving and for §6", and `app_state.rs:331` says "Later tasks use this both for saving and for §6's". `crates/wolf-core/tests/app_state_model.rs:8` and `:238` say the front-ends write fields "only in later tasks", and review 1 §5 named all three. No check caught them: the `changelog` rule of `tools/lint/markdown_check.sh` covers Markdown only and has no "later task" phrase, and doc-lint does not check comment wording.
- **A stale comment the writer saw was left unrouted.** `technical-writer-1`'s report has a section "Stale source comments (no rewrite requested)": the `LEGACY_DB_NAMES` doc at `crates/wolf-core/src/db.rs:33` says "deleted on every `Table::open`", but `AppStateStore` reaches the removal through `Table::open_all`. It landed. This is the pattern of [application-state-3](application-state-3.md) recommendation 1, come up again.
- **Review 1's other wording notes landed as well.** `crates/wolf-ffi/src/lib.rs:2352` says "GNOME's model" of a bridge GNOME does not use. The sentence of `notes/wolf-core-decisions.md:173` that begins "A zoom is dropped for a replaced pane only" reads as if no other rule drops a zoom. `crates/wolf-core/src/README.md:412` has a garbled clause about two handles, and `crates/wolf-core/src/api.rs:1080` has a one-phrase line left by a reflow.
- **The heal guard was not taken up.** [application-state-1](application-state-1.md) recommendation 6 asked to guard the heal in `db.rs` before state tables share `wolf.sqlite`. This task's instructions did not include it, and both `technical-writer-1` and review 1 §6 flagged it. The gotcha at `crates/wolf-core/src/README.md:420` now covers a user's saved windows, and the risk goes live once a front-end opens `AppStateStore`.
- **The word budget was counted by hand.** The `db.rs` and `state.rs` entries of `crates/wolf-core/src/README.md` were already at 250 words, which plan 1 §5 did not count before assigning additions. `technical-writer-1` measured them with `sed … | wc -w` three times and reworked the `db.rs` entry over three scripted edits. `markdown_check.sh --entry-words` exists and `build.md` lists it, but neither the architect's nor the writer's system prompt names it.
- **`crates/wolf-core/tests/README.md` grew past the size warning again.** It went from 62,055 to 65,120 bytes, and `documentation-1` printed it as a `big-file` warning, as [application-state-1](application-state-1.md) and [ghostty-2](ghostty-2.md) recorded for earlier growth. `runtime/nvim/README.md` is still past it at 53,821 bytes.
- **The plan gave two falsifier IDs to a guard it placed twice.** Plan 1 §2 says `project_states` skips `""` as well as `project_tab`, so P6a's and S6a's one disabling edit could not go red. `coder-1` saw this before running and kept one guard, and review 1 §1 accepted it. Nothing in the repository invited it.
- **The writer's shell stalled for five minutes.** `technical-writer-1` ran `cat > "$TMPDIR/x"` with no input, which blocked until the tool's 300 s timeout, then ran `pkill -f 'cat'` to clear it. That was a slip, and nothing in the repository invited it.

## Recommendations

1. **Number panes in one place, and pin it with a nested layout.** In `crates/wolf-core/src/app_state.rs`, make `replace_missing` and `node_spec` take their indices from the order `Node::panes` defines, through one shared walk. Add a case to `crates/wolf-core/tests/app_state_rules.rs` with a missing file and a zoom inside a nested layout followed by a later sibling, as review 1 §3 gap 1 asks. It removes three walks that can drift apart before the front-ends depend on them.
2. **Catch task-series wording mechanically.** Add "later task", "next task" and "this task" to the `changelog` rule of `tools/lint/markdown_check.sh`, and apply the same phrases to comments and doc comments through `tools/doc-lint`, with `notes/` exempt. `git grep -i -e 'later task' -e 'next task' -e 'this task'` outside `notes/`, `.claude/` and `specs/` lists exactly the three lines of this task plus `crates/wolf-gnome/tests/smoke_headless/sidebar.rs:1431`, so the rule would be cheap to adopt and would have failed `implementation-1` here.
3. **Route what an agent sees to its owner.** This is recommendation 1 of [application-state-3](application-state-3.md), 2 of [application-state-2](application-state-2.md) and 2 of [sidebar-toggles-1](sidebar-toggles-1.md), come up a fourth time. `technical-writer-1`'s `LEGACY_DB_NAMES` note is this task's evidence: a `rejected` would have sent it to the coder before the review.
4. **Name `--entry-words` where the budget is planned and spent.** In `.claude/skills/regina/agents/technical-writer.md`, beside the `markdown_check.sh` line, and in the documentation item of `.claude/skills/regina/agents/architect.md`, say to run `./tools/lint/markdown_check.sh --entry-words <file>` before adding to an entry. The flag answers [open-items-14](open-items-14.md) recommendation 2 and [ghostty-4](ghostty-4.md) recommendation 4, but no definition uses it, and this task counted with `wc -w`.
5. **Put the heal guard into the next task of this series.** This is recommendation 6 of [application-state-1](application-state-1.md), come up again. Whoever writes the instructions for the first front-end switch should include it, since that task is when `window_state` and `project_state` start holding a user's windows.
6. **Fix the drift this task left.** Reword `crates/wolf-core/src/app_state.rs:331` and `crates/wolf-core/tests/app_state_model.rs:8` and `:238` to the present tense, make `crates/wolf-core/src/db.rs:33` name `Table::open_all`, and drop "GNOME's" at `crates/wolf-ffi/src/lib.rs:2352`. Rewrite `notes/wolf-core-decisions.md:173` and `crates/wolf-core/src/README.md:412` as review 1 §5 suggests, and join the orphaned line at `crates/wolf-core/src/api.rs:1080`.
