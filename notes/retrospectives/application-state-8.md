# GNOME saves and restores the per-tab and per-pane extras

Last verified at commit `d8650dba26f4ed6e1e0515792c9c48680d1316ee`.

The task made `crates/wolf-gnome` write and restore a tab's `focused`, `sidebar` and `drawer`, an edit pane's `cursor` and `top_line`, and a preview's `scroll`, and moved the drawer share from the window to the tab. It also added `WolfCore::set_window_cursor` and `wolf_set_window_cursor` in `runtime/nvim/init.lua`, which no plan foresaw at the start. The 16 invocations cost $75.00. Three round trips added about $16.50: `coder-1`'s `rejected`, a Linux failure of `implementation-1`, and review 1's rejection.

## What went well

- **The previous task's core API carried the GNOME side.** Plan 1 §1 reused `WolfCore::window_cursor`, `app_state::restored_tab` and `tab_rebuild_spec`, and review 1 §1 found D1 to D7 implemented as written. The GNOME work was seams, capture and restore in `crates/wolf-gnome/src`, not a format change.
- **The pure seams took most of the falsifiers.** `state_seam`, `pane_layout`, `pane_zoom` and `drawer_share` gave E1 to E15 homes in pure test files, and `coder-2` recorded the reds of 68 falsifiers, pure and smoke, for $4.98. Review 1 §4 found every ID with its own red.
- **The wolf-core real-Neovim harness made the new premise testable on both workers.** C1 in `crates/wolf-core/tests/app_state_rebuild.rs` uses the existing `common::start_core_sized` and `WolfCore::resize_grid` to show the re-scroll, then the fix. It needed no new target, because `//crates/wolf-core:app_state_rebuild` already had the Neovim runfiles.
- **The documentation gate passed on its first run both times.** `documentation-1` and `documentation-2` exited 0 on both workers.

## What did not go well

- **A premise cited as covered by a test was not covered.** Plan 1 §6.2 named `a_saved_cursor_and_top_line_restore` as covering the restored top line. That test reads the view straight after the rebuild, before a front-end sizes its grids, and Neovim's default `splitkeep=cursor` then re-scrolls the window. Plan 1's own S1 put the cursor on the top row (`{120, 3}`, top line 120), the one case the re-scroll leaves alone, so no planned test could see it.
- **That premise cost the first round trip.** `coder-1` ($10.90) found it through the divider relaunch smoke and returned `rejected`, with a `splitkeep=topline` experiment as evidence. `architect-2` ($4.56) and `test-writer-2` ($3.38) added `set_window_cursor`, D9's re-application and the tests C1 and S7. No document named the re-scroll before this task; `notes/wolf-core-decisions.md` does now.
- **One smoke's capture depended on where the restored cursor sits, and caused two round trips.** `restore::dragged_divider_weights_survive_relaunch_under_cage` OCRs the left pane's heading, and run 1's probe clicks leave `dvleft.md`'s cursor on its last letter. Restoring that cursor drew the block over the `A`, which was `coder-1`'s second reason. Plan 2's fix typed `0` straight after `<C-w>h`, and `implementation-1` failed on Linux when the key outran the focus intent.
- **The focus-intent rule was known in one place and missed in another.** The `layout.rs` entry of `crates/wolf-core/src/README.md` says a focus move is a pure intent the shell applies later, and plan 3 found the other smokes sleeping `SETTLE` after `<C-w>h`. `crates/wolf-gnome/tests/README.md` § Smoke conventions does not say it, and plan 2 did not apply it. `coder-2` saw the smoke pass alone and in its own Linux gate.
- **The gate's excerpt cut off the evidence.** `gates.sh`'s `print_failure` keeps 40 lines after `---- … stdout ----`. Assertion 3's message spends six of them on prose and then prints `tesseract`'s output one token per blank-line-separated paragraph, so the pane text fell outside the excerpt. `architect-3` ($2.62) inferred the cause from the code, and plan 3 says so; with `test-writer-3` and `coder-3`, the round cost $3.86.
- **S2 passed with the restored preview scroll never applied.** Its only run-2 observable was the scroll the quit saved. Plan 1 D3 has a capture keep its seed while `pending_scroll` waits, so a slot never taken saves exactly what was planted. Review 1 F1 found it, and plan 4 made run 2 wheel the page further; with `technical-writer-2` and `reviewer-2`, that round cost $4.70.
- **E17c did not catch S2's gap because it still took the slot.** Plan 1 worded every falsifier as an edit to the code, and none asked what else could satisfy Q. Review 2 §3 asked that question, and plan 4's E17d is the edit S2 had missed.
- **A hand-kept count went stale in a second note.** `technical-writer-1` removed "nine" and "eight" from `notes/wolf-gnome/crash-and-stall-recovery.md` and one count from `notes/feat-list.md`. It missed "All eight calls a running window issues" in `notes/feat-list.md`, which review 1 F2 named.
- **A rejecting coder's falsifier edits were lost.** `coder-1` wrote a driver of 62 runs in its scratch directory and left the runs for later. `coder-2` could not reach that directory and wrote the 68 edits again from plan prose.
- **The GNOME READMEs are still past their size.** `technical-writer-1` ran 127 turns ($9.49), with dozens of Python edits of `crates/wolf-gnome/src/README.md`. Every entry it touched was at 235 to 250 of 250 words, so each addition was paid for with a cut. The two files are 95,627 and 125,660 bytes.
- **Review 1 left leads that the landed work carries.** A `DropSubtree` close captures nothing (N1), `settle_reroot` can show a stale sidebar page (N2), and no in-source test pins `InputEvent::SetWindowCursor` as state (N3). Plan 2 §0 and review 1 N4 also record that macOS restores through the rebuild alone, so its top line likely drifts the same way, unmeasured.

## Recommendations

1. **Have a plan check that a test it cites as covering a behaviour exercises the new condition.** In `.claude/skills/regina/agents/architect.md`, beside the test plan item, say that a test named as already covering something is read, and the plan states the condition it runs under. A read of `a_saved_cursor_and_top_line_restore` would have shown it runs before any grid resize. It extends recommendation 2 of [application-state-1](application-state-1.md) and of [application-state-6](application-state-6.md), come up again, and would have saved `architect-2` and `test-writer-2`.
2. **Say in the smoke conventions that a relaunch smoke observes the restored state live.** In `crates/wolf-gnome/tests/README.md` § Smoke conventions, add that run 2 reads what it restored from Neovim or the page, as S1 and S7 read `line('w0')`, and not only what the next save writes. A capture keeps a pending seed by design, so a save can echo a restore that never applied. It would have kept review 1 F1 out of plan 1.
3. **Say in the smoke conventions that a key typed after a focus chord can reach the old pane.** Add to the same section that `<C-w>h` and its siblings are intents the shell applies on a later wake, and link the `layout.rs` entry of `crates/wolf-core/src/README.md`. To type into a pane, click it, since the click's `focus_win` and the key share one ordered channel. It would have saved plan 3, `implementation-1`'s Linux run and $3.86.
4. **Keep the divider smoke's OCR dumps inside the gate's excerpt.** In `crates/wolf-gnome/tests/smoke_headless/restore.rs`, assertion 3's message prints `--psm 11` and `--psm 6` after six lines of prose. Print the dumps first and drop their blank lines, so `print_failure`'s 40 lines hold the pane text. `architect-3` would then have read the cause rather than inferred it.
5. **Drop hand-kept counts from notes.** This is recommendation 7 of [round-3-11](round-3-11.md), 3 of [ghostty-4](ghostty-4.md), 5 of [ghostty-5](ghostty-5.md) and 3 of [nerd-font-1](nerd-font-1.md), come up again for the blocking-call inventory. As the user's edit, extend `CLAUDE.md`'s rule against counts in comments to documents, so that a set is described by its members or its rule. The same count stood in two notes, and the one left behind cost review 1 F2.
6. **Have a rejecting coder put its falsifier edits in its report.** In `.claude/skills/regina/agents/coder.md`, say that a coder returning `rejected` with falsifier edits written records each as a diff in its report, which the next coder can read. `coder-1`'s driver stayed in a scratch directory, and `coder-2` rewrote 68 edits from prose.
7. **Measure the macOS top-line drift and close the leads.** A later task restores a macOS edit pane whose cursor is not on the top row, asserts its top line, and applies `WolfCore::set_window_cursor` once the grids are sized if it drifts. The same task can take N1's close capture on GNOME and add `SetWindowCursor` to `state_events()` in `crates/wolf-core/src/input.rs` for N3.
8. **Split the wolf-gnome READMEs.** This is recommendation 4 of [gnome-toolbar-2](gnome-toolbar-2.md) and 4 of [followups-3](followups-3.md), come up again. `technical-writer-1`'s 127 turns went mostly to paying for each addition with a cut in an entry at its budget.
