# Retrospective: find and fix why macOS sometimes does not reopen the last session's files

Last verified at commit `7116ac03b9cd030ebe2312e1f0bc2c0596c3633e`.

The task traced eleven ways a saved session could be lost on macOS, fixed seven with a pure `SessionSeam` write model, a per-window `win_buffer` FFI read and a `wolf_barrier` request that makes `rebuild_layout` return only after its windows are reported, and ruled out the rest. It took three plans. Review 1 rejected the first pass on a tabbed-window close (F1), and `coder-2` sent plan 2 back on a swift-format finding in a test file and on R1's detection rate. Both gates passed on both workers at every run, `implementation-1` and `-2`, `documentation-1` and `-2`.

The thirteen invocations cost $33.76. The first pass, `architect-1` through `reviewer-1`, cost $24.37, of which `architect-1` alone was $9.45 over 95 turns. The F1 round cost $7.77 and the swift-format round $1.62.

## What went well

- **wolf-gnome's documented write model was a port target.** `notes/wolf-gnome-decisions.md` "Application state" and `crates/wolf-gnome/src/state_seam.rs` gave plan 1 its refuse-and-cache gather, its seeds and its three close answers. `architect-1` read `close_commit` and `LayoutGather` there instead of designing them. Keep the GNOME note's level of detail: it turned an open hunt into a port.
- **`specs/architecture.md` states that the `windows` map covers the current tabpage only.** That one sentence let plan 1 find C3a, a background view persisting every leaf as `""`. R2 in `crates/wolf-ffi/tests/win_buffer.rs` then pinned the fix.
- **The seam pattern made F1's fix cheap to test.** `CloseBurst` copied `PickerRefreshCoalescer`'s shape of injected `schedule`, `read` and `commit` closures. `test-writer-2` wrote T15–T17 in 11 turns for $0.61, and each of their four falsifiers failed in `coder-2`'s run.
- **A sibling target was there to copy.** The `win_buffer` `rust_test` in `crates/wolf-ffi/BUILD.bazel` is a copy of `tabpages`, and review 1 found every wiring present on the first pass.
- **The previous round's retrospective gave the startup-open lead.** The instructions cited the `history.rs` race from [retro-fixes-3](retro-fixes-3.md), and plan 1's C5c and its barrier came straight from it.

## What did not go well

- **F1: the port counted tabs where GNOME counts windows.** GNOME's `close_commit` counts other live windows, and a GNOME window holds its tabs. On macOS each tab is its own `NSWindow`, and the close button of a tab group closes them one by one, so plan 1's rule kept only the last tab. No document at the base said which macOS unit a GNOME window maps to, or how a tab group closes: `notes/wolf-macos-decisions.md` did not mention tab groups at all. Review 1 caught it by reading; the round cost `architect-2` through `reviewer-2`, $7.77.
- **The glue that hid F1 has no automated test, and its premise still has none.** Plan 1 and plan 2 left `AppDelegate.closeCommit(for:)` and the burst wiring compile-verified only, and review 1 said F1 "is exactly the kind of gap that glue hides". `CloseBurst` rests on AppKit closing a group's windows within one main-queue turn. Review 2 names that premise as unmeasured, and only manual checks 5 and 6 cover it.
- **`architect-2` searched the web for an AppKit fact the worker could have measured.** It made two `WebSearch` and five `WebFetch` calls (Ghostty's `TabGroupCloseCoordinator.swift`, an Apple forum thread, Apple's help) and declined review 1's offscreen measurement, citing the task's falsifier rule. [round-3-7](round-3-7.md) records that Xcode's `swiftc` runs scratch AppKit programs in the worker, but no document says so. The instructions ask a falsifier "for each fix", not for a premise measurement, so the rule was read more broadly than written.
- **The swift-format fixer does not apply a rule the checker enforces.** `test-writer-2` wrote `pending.forEach { $0() }` in `SessionSeamTests.swift` and ran `:swift_format.fix`, as its definition says. `swift-format format` left the line, `lint --strict` failed it under `ReplaceForEachWithForLoop`, and `coder-2` found it only in its own `./gates.sh` run. The file is the test writer's, so a one-line fix took `architect-3`, `test-writer-3` and `coder-3`, $1.62 and seven minutes.
- **R1 is a probabilistic test, and measuring it took three rounds of stress runs.** With the barrier removed, three rounds caught it in 3 of 20 and 5 of 50 macOS runs (`coder-1`), which became review 1's F2. At 50 rounds macOS caught it in 13 of 20, then 20 of 20, and plan 2's bar of 15 of 20 sent the rate to `architect-3`, who accepted it. `coder-1` and `coder-2` together ran about 200 stressed runs across both workers to measure it.
- **Four agents read `nvim-rs` 0.9.2's `neovim.rs` four ways.** `architect-1` used `WebFetch` on docs.rs, `coder-1` found it under Bazel's `external`, and `technical-writer-1` and `reviewer-1` each found the cargo registry. Each checked the same `io_loop` and `handler_loop` arms. The barrier's reason is now in `notes/wolf-core-decisions.md`, which spares the next reader the why but not the where.
- **The `WolfMacOS` entries had no words to spare.** `technical-writer-1` found the `AppDelegate`, `PaneContainerView`, `Project` and `WolfWindowController` entries at exactly 250 words, so every addition was paid for by a cut, and `technical-writer-2` cut three more sentences. `WolfKeyMap/README.md` ended at 60511 bytes and `WolfMacOS/README.md` at 51284, both over the checker's 50000-byte warning.
- **Two notes on the landed work, from review 2.** The two-line comment above `let commit` in `WolfWindowController.teardown()` says the order makes "the last-view count" include the view, which is wrong: `closeCommit(for:)` excludes it by identity. A main-thread stall could put two separate tab closes in one burst, so a tab the user closed would return at relaunch.

## Recommendations

1. **Say which macOS unit a GNOME window is.** In `notes/wolf-macos-decisions.md`, add one paragraph: a GNOME window corresponds to a native tab group, a GNOME tab to a macOS view with its own `NSWindow`, and a tab group's close button closes each tab's window in turn. A rule ported from wolf-gnome that counts windows must count tab groups. That paragraph would have let plan 1 write `CloseBurst` and saved the F1 round, $7.77.
2. **Measure the tab-group close premise in `WolfMacOSTests`.** Add a case to `crates/wolf-macos/Tests/WolfMacOSTests/` that builds a two-window tab group in never-shown windows, clicks its close button, and asserts every window got `windowWillClose` before the main queue next drains. It may turn out that AppKit will not close an offscreen tab group, and the attempt settles that too. It pins the one premise `CloseBurst` rests on, which reviews 1 and 2 both left unmeasured.
3. **Document that the worker can run AppKit scratch programs.** In `build.md`'s macOS section, or the `WolfMacOSTests` README's environment paragraph, say that Xcode's `swiftc` compiles and runs a scratch AppKit program offscreen in the worker. [round-3-7](round-3-7.md) found it by accident, and `architect-2` spent seven web calls on a fact it could have measured.
4. **End the formatting step with the checker.** In `.claude/skills/regina/agents/test-writer.md` and `coder.md`, after the `:swift_format.fix` line, have the agent run `xcrun swift-format lint --strict --follow-symlinks <file>` on each Swift file it wrote. Say in `tools/lint/README.md`'s swift-format bullet that `format` does not apply lint-only rules such as `ReplaceForEachWithForLoop`. Plan 3 added exactly that step for `test-writer-3`, which passed clean; written into the definition, it would have saved $1.62 and one round.
5. **Make R1's falsifier deterministic.** In `crates/wolf-core/tests/rebuild_layout.rs`, have R1 delay Neovim's scheduled callbacks before the rebuild, for example by queueing a `vim.schedule` that sleeps, so that a removed barrier should read the startup `Untitled.md` every time. That would remove `BARRIER_ROUNDS`, review 1's F2, plan 2's threshold, the second item of `coder-2`'s rejection, and about 200 stress runs.
6. **Say where a pinned crate's source is.** This came up again: it is recommendation 4 of [open-items-13](open-items-13.md), and [open-items-7](open-items-7.md) saw the same fetch. Here four agents found `nvim-rs` four ways, one of them over the network.
7. **Split the `WolfMacOS` README.** This came up again: it is recommendation 4 of [round-3-7](round-3-7.md). In this task four entries sat at the 250-word budget, and both technical writers had to cut before they could add.
